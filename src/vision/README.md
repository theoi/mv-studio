# src/vision

Vision AI in workers: person masks for "text behind the subject", click-to-select objects, footage matching (CLIP), motion energy, subject tracking for auto-reframe, and depth for 2.5D camera moves. Owner: vision area.

See AGENTS.md for the shared contracts (edit API, job runtime, model loader).

## Using it

```ts
import { createVisionService, createWorkerBackends, createOpfsCache, fromJob } from '../vision/index.ts'

const vision = createVisionService({ backends: createWorkerBackends(), cache: createOpfsCache() })
jobs.run({
  kind: 'vision.mask',
  title: 'Finding the subject',
  run: (ctx) => vision.personTrack(source, fromJob(ctx)),
})
```

- `livePreview()`: person mask per playback frame (pass ~256 px frames; ~9 ms per frame). Drops stale frames instead of lagging.
- `checkSuitability(source)`: `{ verdict, reasons, suitableRanges }` for the UI ("subject too small", "no subject", ...). Takes about 2–4 s per shot. It judges with the same rules as the person track's report (`personTrack(...).suitability`, `assessSuitability` in `mask/suitability.ts`), so the verdict before and after cutting out agree: on 60 of 65 dev clips (not in the repository), up from 40; the other 5 differ only because the track also measures flicker. A track report stored by an older version is judged again from its stored masks once and stored again (`SUITABILITY_VERSION`); a track stored before person detection first gets a detection-only pass, and one stored before pop shares a pass that decodes each frame for its luma (neither segments again). Each report keeps what it judged (`evidence`: every judged frame's verdict and the frames that pop), so the app judges each timeline clip on the part of the footage it shows (`suitabilityInRange`): a clip of the part with a large person is offered although the rest of the shot is a wide view, and a clip of the wide part is not. The quick check offers a part only where its person is usable for at least 1.2 s, not the track's 1 s (`CHECK_MIN_RANGE_SECONDS`, kept in its evidence so each clip's part is judged with it too): its rougher masks, sampled at other times, often put a part just over 1 s under it by the track. The minimum allows half the spacing between samples (`checkMinRangeSeconds`), so a shot over 18 s, sampled less than 5 times a second, needs the sample count nearest 1.2 s rather than the next one up (one sample at 1 a second). The dev clips are all 5.5 to 8 s long, so long shots are not measured. On 2 to 4 s parts of the 65 dev clips, parts the check offers and the track then turns down go from 10 to 5 of 1413, and parts the track would offer but the check does not go from 14 to 16; no whole-clip verdict changes. The app says a part-only offer from the check may still be turned down.
- Flicker (`unstable-mask`) is measured only on the track (`mask/pops.ts`), on every frame after the temporal filter: the share of the subject that switches off and back on, or on and off, within 0.125 s on each side. It counts only where the frame's luma changes by less than 16, so a person who moves does not count, and only in blocks that survive a 3 × 3 opening at half size, so the outline moving out by a pixel or two and back does not count (that shimmer weighed most on small subjects). The track file stores it per frame (`meta.pops`). A frame pops visibly at 5 % of the subject (`POP_MIN_SHARE`), and the mask is unstable when more than one frame in ten does (`minStability` 0.9). On the 65 dev clips this turns 4 of the 20 good usable clips marginal, each with parts that visibly blink in and out of the cutout (a patch of graffiti floor, a patch of wall, neighbours, a skater's dark trousers), and one wide shot whose cutout takes in bystanders. The closest usable clip that stays good is at 0.931 (a passer-by blinking in front of a busker), and the highest flagged one at 0.894 (one pop frame fewer and the skater would stay good). Both limits and the opening were chosen on these clips.
- Flicker blind spots: a dropout or a false part that lasts 0.25 s or more (6 frames at 24 fps) is not counted at all, and shorter ones count on fewer frames than they last; a false part that joins the cutout and stays counts only through its own flicker. About one visible blink every 2 s is tolerated: a busker whose shin drops out for 2 or 3 frames about three times in 6 s stays good. A shot marginal only for flicker has a person throughout, so it says that the cutout flickers, not that it works in parts, and the cutout says so when it lands. The meter takes about 0.18 s for a 6 s shot at the track's 512 px mask size (0.05 s without the opening), against about 13 s for the whole track (median of 25 shots on a 4-core VM without a GPU).
- Person evidence: the person segmenter also cuts out animals and some vehicles, so both verdicts count only the mask inside the boxes of people an object detector finds (MediaPipe EfficientDet-Lite0, 4.6 MB, in the segmentation worker). Boxes need a score of at least `PERSON_MIN_SCORE` (0.3; the detector keeps boxes down to `DETECTOR_MIN_SCORE`, 0.2, and the track stores each box's score, so another limit down to 0.2 can be tried on stored tracks with `minPersonScore` without detecting again), grow by 15 % on each side, and fill gaps: a frame with people found at most 2.5 detection intervals before and after it (0.5 s at 5 a second; one side is enough near the start or end) keeps their boxes, so a person the detector misses once or twice in a row stays at any frame rate, while an animal it takes for a person now and then counts only in that frame. The quick check looks in every sample and the track 5 times a second; the track file stores the boxes (`people`). The detector adds about 0.5 s to the quick check of a 6 s shot (2.2 to 2.7 s) and about 0.5 s to its track (12.6 to 13.1 s), medians of 25 shots on a 4-core VM without a GPU.
- Animals as negative evidence: the same detector run also reports the COCO animal classes (bird, cat, dog, horse, sheep, cow, elephant, bear, zebra, giraffe; each frame keeps up to 10 people and 10 animals), and the frames store them (`animals`, `animalScores`). The detector's classes share one non-max suppression, so it names each object once per frame: a cat it takes for a person now and then is a cat in its other frames, never a cat box beside the person box. So a person box counts only while the same object (boxes overlapping by an IoU of 0.5 or more) is called a person at least as often as an animal within 3 s of it (`ANIMAL_VOTE_SECONDS`, at least 15 detection intervals on a long shot's quick check). On the 65 dev clips, this turns the cat close-up (a person in 5 of its 29 track detections, a cat in 24) from marginal to unsuitable in both the quick check and the track, and changes no other verdict: no-person shots offered go from 1 of 27 to 0. On every 2, 3 and 4 s part of the clips, parts with no person that the check offers go from 6 to 0 of 1413, and parts the check offers and the track then turns down from 7 to 1. A shorter vote, over 1 s, let a guitarist's run of "animal" frames (10 of his 29) outvote him, good to marginal. Asking for the animal classes also changes some low-score person boxes (the shared suppression), in 21 of the 65 clips, with no verdict change. Rabbits and goats are not COCO classes, so a close-up of one is not caught. A person whose box a large animal or a costume mostly covers (hugging a big dog in a close-up, a mascot) can be ruled out where the detector calls them an animal more often. People stored by an older version are detected again once (`PEOPLE_VERSION`).
- Measured on the 65 dev clips in the production build: of 27 clips without a person, the track and the quick check each called 0 good and 1 marginal, a cat close-up (before: 10 and 4 on the track, 11 and 5 on the quick check), and none since the animal vote above. Of 25 clips with a usable person, 24 keep their verdicts; a skater who is large only in the last third drops from marginal to unsuitable. Of 13 wide shots with small people, 12 are unsuitable (before: 11). The score, hold and margin were chosen on these same clips, so a fresh set is the real test. The cutout keeps only the parts of the mask near these people (cutout parts, below).
- Measured on a fresh set of 70 clips the builders had not seen (40 with people, in 14 of which the people fill under 3 % of the frame, an approximate split; 30 without a person), against the build before the quick check's 1.2 s parts, the animal vote, `no-person-found` and the cutout parts: of the 30 clips without a person, none is rated good and 1 is offered as marginal, on both checks. 5 of the 70 verdicts change, all toward unsuitable: 2 clips without a person, and 3 of the 40 with people (2 where the people fill under 3 % of the frame, and 1 where a half-hidden person fills 11 % and is no longer found). Of the 14 wide shots, 10 are unsuitable on both checks (before: 8 and 9). The quick check and the track disagree on 3 of the 70 (before: 6). The four changes were measured together, so this does not tell which one changed each verdict.
- Without the detectors: when the person detector cannot be downloaded or loaded (offline, a blocked host), the quick check, the track and the judging of a stored track go on without it. The verdict then counts the whole mask, animals included, the cutout keeps the whole mask until then, and the verdict says so (`peopleSkipped`; the app adds "Couldn’t check for people, so the cutout may include animals or objects. It checks again when you’re back online."), and the people are looked for again the next time the track is judged. A face detector that cannot load is skipped the same way (`facesSkipped`: observations without faces, which subject tracking does not reuse). The segmenter model and the MediaPipe runtime are still needed, cached or downloadable.
- Suitability limits: the segmenter, and the detector (which sees a 320 px image), find nobody both in empty shots and in wide shots where people are small, so `no-subject` ("No one in this shot is large enough to cut out") can mean either. A frame with no one found near it, although the segmenter cut out a shape large enough for the effect (one that passes the size rules, or fills the frame), is `no-person-found` instead ("Couldn’t find a person to cut out in this shot"): an animal, a vehicle, or a person the detector missed. Someone found elsewhere in the frame keeps it at `no-subject`. It changes only the reason a frame gives, and stored track reports are judged again once from their stored masks and people (`SUITABILITY_VERSION` 7, no new detection). On the 73 dev clips (the quick check from the production build, the track judged again from stored track masks and people), 13 of the 27 clips without a person now give this reason first on the track and 12 on the quick check: 11 animal shots, a plane and a dog walk (track only). The other 14 still say no one is large enough first, since in most of their frames the segmenter cuts out nothing or only small shapes. Of the 31 clips with a usable person, one changes, on the quick check only: a skater the detector misses in half its samples, unsuitable either way. None of the 15 wide shots of small people changes. On these clips no verdict or suitable range changes, on the 146 whole-clip judgements or on 3162 parts of 2, 3 and 4 s. On the fresh set above, measured together with the other changes, 3 of the 40 clips with people do change verdict.
- `personTrack(source, { hqRanges })`: export mask track (512 px, output fps, edge-refined, zero-lag temporal filter, only the parts near the people found, MODNet matte inside `hqRanges`). Cached per media id and options.
- Cutout parts: the track keeps only the parts of each mask (4-connected regions of 128 and up) that reach into a person box, grown by 15 % (`keepPersonParts`), with the verdict's score (0.3) and animal vote. An animal, a vehicle or a patch of background apart from the people goes, while an arm or hair outside the box stays with its body, and soft edge pixels within 2 px of a kept part stay. Boxes are held for 1 s across detector misses (`CUTOUT_HOLD_SECONDS`, longer than the verdict's 0.5 s, since a person missing from the cutout for a moment pops in front of the text). Past the hold, a frame with no one found keeps the parts that overlap the previous frame's cutout, for up to 2 s (`CUTOUT_CARRY_SECONDS`). Without the detector the masks stay whole until the people are found when the track is next judged; the stored masks are then cut down the same way, from every frame decoded again but not segmented. In the app, the project records how each person mask video is made from now on (`vision.person-masks`: the track version, and whether no one was looked for). Masks made before these records have none, so they count as an earlier version: one made without the detector is not recognized as such, is not made again and shows no note. A recorded mask video made without the detector that a cutout uses is made again once the detector can be downloaded (back online, when a project opens, after another cutout, or when an undo or redo brings back a cutout on it), and every cutout that used it gets the new one (one on a locked track once it is unlocked). Until then the cutout keeps its note that it may include animals or objects, whatever a newer check says. The old mask video stays in the project until the project is next opened, since an undo can bring back a cutout that used it, which then gets the new mask straight away. A mask video from an earlier version stays in the cutouts that already use it, since changing saved work silently is worse. Applying the cutout again makes a new one. A mask waits until the detections that can change it are in, 4 s of frames later (`peopleIndexReach`: the hold plus the 3 s animal vote), so the stored masks, their coverage and the pop meter run 4 s behind the decoded frames, and the end of the track stores the rest. The track runs at 30 fps at most, so that queue holds up to 120 masks of 512 px with their luma: about 35 MB for 16:9 or 9:16 footage (512 × 288), 47 MB for 4:3 and 63 MB for square footage. Subject tracking's observations (`personBox`, `personCoverage`) come from the kept masks. The verdict's frames (every 0.2 s) that the cutout cuts down from a shape large enough for the effect are noted in the track (`largeBeforeGate`, about 0.2 ms a frame), so a shot of an animal says no person was found (`no-person-found`) on the track as in the quick check, although its cutout is empty: on the 65 dev clips, every judged frame of the track gets the same verdict as on the uncut masks. Tracks stored by an older version are built again once (`PERSON_TRACK_VERSION`).
- Measured on the 65 dev clips in the production build: in shots without a person, the cutout covers 1.5 % of the frame on average, down from 11.4 %, and clips with a usable person keep 98.5 % of theirs. In wide shots with small people (unsuitable anyway) it keeps 71 %, since the detector misses many of them. No verdict changes on these clips (on the fresh set above, measured together with the other changes, 3 of the 40 clips with people do); the pop meter now measures the kept masks, and the most a usable clip's stability drops is from 0.993 to 0.972. What goes in usable clips is mostly false parts: a title card, a parked car, a pillar, a bird puppet (by the animal vote), a ceiling light, puddle reflections and a skater's shadow. It also drops people the detector scores under 0.3, whom the verdict does not count either: a small skater far away for the first 0.71 s of a shot, another skater for 4 frames (0.17 s), a busker in a costume for the first frame and part of a bystander for 2.8 s. With a score of 0.2, all four stay (usable clips keep 99.3 %), but shots without a person keep 2.1 % and the bird puppet stays. Building a track takes about as long as before: on 12 of the clips, the median build (13 to 22 s per clip) took 0.1 s longer, less than the 1 to 1.5 s a clip varies between runs.
- Cutout limits: a false part that touches a person (a dog held in the arms, a pillar a skater brushes past) stays in the cutout, and one that touches them only now and then blinks in and out (the pillar is kept in a few frames of its shot).
- `selectionTrack(source, { t, points }, { corrections })`: object the user clicked, followed through the clip, with per-frame states and correction clicks (see below).
- `subjects(source)`: subject track for auto-reframe (see below).
- `indexClip(source)` + `matcher.rank(library, { text, mood, energy })`: footage ranking for lyric lines. `createDirectorFootageMatcher` adapts this to the director's interface. For the app, `createFootageService` (below) analyzes imports in the background and searches the library.
- `depth.get({ mediaId, quality, sourceSize, frame })`: Depth Anything V2 Small depth map, cached per media id and quality. `addCameraMove` builds on it (see below).

Frames come from a `FrameSource` (`runtime/frames.ts`). Use `createAssetFrameSource(asset, mediaStore)` for stored assets: it decodes through the engine's export decoder, so analysis sees exactly the exported pixels, and it is about 4x faster than seeking a `<video>`. `createVideoElementSource` covers blobs that are not stored yet.

MediaPipe tasks run only in the segmentation workers, and only through `createMediaPipeTask` (`runtime/loaders.ts`), which first makes the worker's `fetch` answer Google's private API hosts itself (`blockTelemetry`, `runtime/telemetry.ts`). MediaPipe Tasks 1.2.0 posts usage metrics (the task type and its latencies, with the browser's IP address and User-Agent) to odml.pa.googleapis.com from every task, 60 s after the task is created and when it closes. It has no setting to turn this off, and its maintainers' advice is to block the host. The guard answers with 403, which MediaPipe's logger takes as a failed send: it drops its queue and stops, retrying and logging nothing. Each blocked request leaves a `mv-studio:blocked-request` performance mark in the worker. `tests/e2e/mediapipe-telemetry.spec.ts` runs a cutout and an auto-reframe export and checks that no request reaches those hosts.

Clip thumbnails come from `createFrameThumbnails(media)` (`runtime/thumbnails.ts`): 160×90 JPEG frames of a video at a source time, rounded to 0.5 s, as object URLs (revoked when a frame is dropped). Callers `want(asset, time)` while a frame is shown and release it after; the newest request goes first and released requests are skipped. Frames are decoded and scaled in a worker (`openVideoFrames`, WebCodecs through mediabunny, `workers/frames.worker.ts`), with the container rotation and the color tagging the preview's `<video>` uses (untagged video under 720 lines is BT.601, as in the engine's export decoder), so the page only draws the small frame and the JPEG encodes asynchronously (`coverJpeg`); a file the worker cannot read falls back to a `<video>` on the page. Importing twelve 1080p clips with the preview playing costs the page about 3 ms in total for their Library thumbnails, down from about 280 ms (a 1080p canvas copy per clip), on headless Chromium without a GPU. The app's `useThumbnail(asset)` (Library cards, 320×180) and `useFrameThumbnail(asset, clip.sourceStart)` (`src/app/state/thumbs.ts`) wrap it.

## Click-to-select tracking

```ts
const first = { t: 2, points: [{ x: 0.19, y: 0.48 }] }
const sel = await vision.selectionTrack(source, first, fromJob(ctx))
// The user clicked the object again where it was lost:
const fixed = await vision.selectionTrack(source, first, {
  ...fromJob(ctx),
  corrections: [{ t: 0.5, points }],
})
const blob = await encodeMaskVideo(fixed.track, { duration }) // then a clip matte, as for person masks
```

- Each mask frame (10 per second) predicts where the object is from the camera motion and the object's own speed, prompts MagicTouch on points inside that prediction (moved onto the object's own colours when something covers part of it) and keeps the result only if it agrees in place, size and colour with the clicked object. It does not slide onto a neighbour it merges with or onto someone who walks in front; those frames are `lost`, and the neighbour's colours count as background from then on. A lost object is searched for near where it should be and picked up again when it reappears. About 0.23 s per mask frame on CPU (an 8 s clip in about 19 s).
- States per mask frame in `states` (with `confidence`, 0..1), as runs in `spans` (`{ start, end, state }` in media seconds, for a strip under the clip), and at a time with `selectionStateAt(result, t)`:
  - `tracking`: found.
  - `uncertain`: found, but partly hidden, just picked up again or a weak match.
  - `lost`: expected in view but not found. The first 0.2 s keep the predicted mask so a dropout does not flicker; after that the mask is empty, so nothing is cut out where the object is not.
  - `out-of-frame`: the object left the picture; empty mask. Nothing to fix.
- Corrections: `needsCorrection` lists the times worth a second click (the start of each `lost` run and of `uncertain` runs of 0.5 s or more), e.g. "Lost at 0:01.1 · click it to fix". Pass every later click as `corrections`; each re-anchors the track: frames up to halfway to the neighbouring clicks follow from it, and further where the neighbour had lost the object. Results are cached per set of clicks.
- Limits: prompts are positive points, so two objects touching at a click (two people walking together) are selected together, also by a correction.
- Review on real footage: `SELECTION_QUALITY=1 npx playwright test tests/e2e/selection-quality.spec.ts` writes contact sheets (mask tinted by state, IoU between frames) and export renders with text behind the selection.

## Footage library: search, suggestions, background analysis

```ts
const footage = createFootageService({ editor, vision, media: appMedia })
footage.indexing.start() // analyzes clips and stills as they are imported
const state = useSyncExternalStore(footage.indexing.subscribe, footage.indexing.getState)
const results = await footage.search('night city')
```

- `search(text)`, `suggestForLine(lineId)` and `suggestForSection(sectionId)` return `{ assetId, name, kind, score, range }`, best first. `score` is 0..1; `score >= STRONG_MATCH` counts as a match ("3 clips match"). `range` is the best-matching part of the clip in source seconds, for the hover preview and for placing the clip. Line suggestions use the line's text and the song's energy while it is sung; section suggestions use the section's first lyric lines, its energy and a mood from it. Clips that are not analyzed yet are not returned.
- Background analysis: one job (`FOOTAGE_INDEX_JOB` = `'vision.index'`, "Analyzing footage", message "Clip 3 of 10 · name") analyzes imported and generated clips and stills one at a time, oldest first, and picks up clips imported while it runs. Each clip gets two steps: the matching index (stage `indexing`), then subject tracking for auto-reframe (stage `subjects`; 5 samples per second, faces first, else person masks; one sample for a still). Clips analyzed before subject tracking existed get only the second step. Each result is cached per clip and written to the project's visual analysis with `history: 'skip'` as soon as its step finishes: saved and in the portable file, but never an undo step. The director and auto-cut read the matching index, and export's auto framing (`reframePlanFor`, `convertToFormat`) reads the subject track. The job's `fraction` covers both steps of every clip; `state.current` is `{ assetId, name, fraction, step }`, with `fraction` covering that clip's steps.
- `media` must resolve every asset the project can hold: in the app, `appMedia` (`src/app/state/media.ts`), which also serves the sample project's generated files.
- Cancel (`indexing.cancel()`) pauses analysis until `resume()` or the next import. A clip that fails to decode is skipped and listed in `state.failed`; a clip whose subject tracking fails keeps its matching index. A failure that would hit every clip (model download, out of memory) stops the run with `status: 'error'`.
- UI states. Library badge per clip from `statusOf(assetId)`: `queued`, `indexing`, `indexed` (searchable), `failed` (message in `state.failed`; "Retry" calls `resume({ retryFailed: true })`), `paused` ("Resume"), `unsupported` (audio, fonts, derived media). Search box: while nothing is analyzed, "Analyzing your footage… results appear as clips finish"; with no results, "No clips match". The activity tray shows the job with Cancel. `status: 'error'` shows `state.error.message` with Retry (`resume()`).
- Framing status per clip from `subjectStatusOf(assetId)`, for the export dialog: `analyzed` (auto framing follows the subject), `no-subject` (tracked and nobody found, so a centred crop is right), `analyzing` (the current clip; progress in `state.current.fraction`), `queued`, `paused`, `failed` (cropped in the centre), `unsupported`. Only `queued`, `paused`, `analyzing` and `failed` clips are cropped in the centre for lack of analysis.

## Auto-cut (Assist › Auto-cut)

```ts
const handle = jobs.run({
  kind: AUTOCUT_JOB,
  title: 'Cutting to the beat',
  run: (ctx) =>
    autoCut({ editor, matcher: vision.matcher }, { pace: 'bar', followEnergy: true }, fromJob(ctx)),
})
const { stats, warnings, label } = await handle.promise
```

- **Options:** `pace` ("Cut every": `beat`, `2-beats`, `bar`, `2-bars`, `phrase`); `followEnergy` (pace by song structure, with `pace` as the verse pace); `cutOn` (`beats`, `downbeats` for bar starts only, `sections` for section boundaries only); `order` (`matched` for "Match clips to the mood", `shuffled`, `chronological` for story order); `range` (whole song, a section, or the timeline selection); `assetIds` (which clips; default all); `seed` ("Try another").
- **Rhythm:** cuts come from the beat grid with the director's planner helpers, never shorter than 0.35 s, so auto-cut and the director cut alike. With `followEnergy`, each section's pace comes from its role (`paceFor` in `src/director/rules.ts`): choruses a step faster than the chosen pace, intros, bridges and outros a step slower, other sections by their energy, and energy moves a role one step at most. Inside a section the cuts are phrased bar by bar (`phrasedCutTimes` in `src/director/timing.ts`): a bar clearly louder than the rest of the section cuts twice as often, a clearly quieter one half as often, the last bar speeds up into a faster section, and shots of a bar or more start on bar lines. On three real songs (`tests/unit/vision/autocut/songs`) verses run 1.5–2.8 s per shot and choruses 0.9–1.6 s, with 60–70 % of cuts on bar lines. With `sections`, a section longer than any clip is split on bar lines.
- **Clip choice (matched):** each shot is ranked for the lyric line sung during it and the song's energy at that moment. Variety comes next: no clip twice in a row, recently used clips score lower, unused ones higher, and the best match for a lyric that is about to start is kept for it. Each shot takes the clip's best-matching range and avoids ranges used before. A clip a bit shorter than its shot plays slower, down to 0.5x. Clips without analysis are still used, picked for variety, with a warning (worded by the caller's `unanalyzedWarning` when it knows why, such as analysis that stopped or couldn't read a clip). The plan records why each clip was chosen (`reason`: `lyric`, `brief`, `energy`, `variety`, `story`).
- **Applying:** one dispatch, so one undo step labeled `Auto-cut: 72 cuts`. Clips go on an "Auto-cut" track above the topmost footage, or, in a project made from a starter template, on the template's Video track while it is empty, unlocked and shown (`templateState(project).tracks.video`). A track that holds earlier auto-cut clips is the one later runs use, whatever its name. Clips are muted (the song is the soundtrack) and carry `meta.autocut` with a fingerprint. Running again replaces only auto-cut clips nobody touched in the range. The range grows to whole earlier auto-cut clips. Clips the user made or edited on the track stay, and the new cut goes around them. If the Auto-cut track is locked, it is kept and the new cut goes on a new track above it. No other track is touched.
- **Deterministic:** the same project, options and seed give the same cut (`planAutoCut` returns the shots without applying them; `applyAutoCut` applies a plan).
- **For planners:** `chooseClips({ spans, pool: autoCutPool(project), order, lyrics, energyAt, matcher, seed })` picks clips for shots a planner cut itself. Each span can carry hints from a brief. `text` is matched together with the lyric line sung then, `mood` goes to the matcher, and `prefer` lists clips that score higher but still never play twice in a row. Picks made for a hint have `reason: 'brief'`.
- **UI states:** `idle` shows the options. "Cut to the beat" is disabled with a hint when there is no song or no clips; while footage is still being analyzed, a note says matching improves when it finishes. `running` shows the job's progress (`analyzing` first when started with `analyzeFirst`, then `planning` and `matching`) with Cancel. `done` shows `stats.cuts`, the track it is on (`track.name`) and `warnings`, with Undo (`editor.undo()` while `editor.undoLabel() === label`), Keep and Try another (next seed; replaces the untouched cuts). `error` shows the `JobError` message: no song, no clips, or nothing to cut in the range.

## Results in the project

`buildVisualAnalysis()` produces `VisualAnalysis` (`version: 'vision-1'`) for `analysis.setVisual`: int8 embeddings, motion and shot changes, subjects, suitability. `readClipIndex`, `readSubjectTrack` and `projectLibrary` read it back and return null for missing or foreign data. Mask tracks are binary (`serializeTrack`, RLE frames in a small container) and are stored as derived media.

## Auto-reframe

The subject track is plain data at `project.analysis.visual[assetId].data.subjects`, written with `analysis.setVisual(buildVisualAnalysis({ assetId, duration, sourceSize, subjects }, previous))`.

- **Time base:** asset source seconds (the time used to decode the clip's frame), so one track serves every clip and trim of the asset. 5 samples per second by default.
- **Samples:** `{ t, fx, fy, box, c, src }` in normalized source coordinates with a top-left origin. `fx, fy` is the point to keep in frame (the largest face, else the upper body of the person mask). `box` is `[x, y, width, height]` around all people, or null. `c` is the 0..1 confidence. `src` is `'face' | 'person' | 'selection' | 'none'`. `readSubjectTrack(analysis)` returns them typed.
- **Smoothing:** already applied to focus and box (5-sample median, then a Gaussian with sigma 0.25 s).
- **No subject:** samples have `src: 'none'` and `c: 0`. Their focus holds the last subject for 1 s, then eases to the next one, or stays centred if the clip has nobody. A subject seen for less than 0.6 s (or a quarter of the clip) counts as noise and is stored as `none`, so a face detected in one frame of a street scene does not steer the framing of the whole clip.

For a virtual camera, call `reframePlanFor(asset, analysis, aspect, options?)` once per asset, aspect (`'16:9' | '9:16' | '1:1' | '4:5'`) and options, then `cropAt(plan, sourceTime)` per frame. `cropAt` returns `{ x, y, width, height }` in normalized source units (top-left origin, target aspect, always inside the source). The plan adds a dead zone (12 % of the crop), a speed limit (0.6 crop widths per second), easing, and hard cuts at the shot changes stored by footage indexing (`options.cuts` overrides them). `options.zoom` (>= 1) crops tighter. Without subject data the plan is a static centred crop. Both functions are pure and synchronous, so preview and export frame identically.

## Compositing masks

`encodeMaskVideo(track, { duration })` turns a mask track into a small grayscale video (VP9 WebM, else H.264) aligned to source time. Store it with `mediaStore.storeBlob(blob, maskAssetInfo(host, blob))` and set `clip.matte = behindSubjectMatte(mask.id, host.id, feather)` on the text layer: the compositor's matte then hides the text where the subject is, the same way in preview and export.

`MASK_REFINE_GLSL` / `createMaskRefinePass` refine a low-resolution mask to output resolution with the frame as guide (GPU version of `mask/upsample.ts`).

## 3D camera moves on stills

`addCameraMove({ editor, media: mediaStore, depth: vision.depth }, clipId, { preset, intensity, ...fromJob(ctx) })` is the "Add 3D camera move" action. It estimates the still's depth (Depth Anything V2 Small, cached; about 4–8 s on CPU the first time), stores it as a derived grayscale PNG (reused on later calls without estimating again, and then the focus and the smear prediction come from that image; its asset records the preparation that made it in its provider, `DEPTH_PREPARATION_VERSION`, and one from an older preparation is prepared again from the cached estimate, or a new one when the cache has none, while older depth images of the still that no effect uses any more are removed in the same step), and adds the `camera-3d` effect (`src/plugins/parallax.ts`) first on the clip with `progress` keyframed from 0 to 1 across the clip, replacing an earlier camera move. Everything lands in one dispatch, so it is one undo step.

- `addCameraMoves(deps, clipIds, options)` does the same for several image clips in one dispatch (one undo step, labeled `Add 3D camera moves to 3 images`, returned as `label`). Clips of the same still share one depth estimate. Assist › 3D camera uses it.
- Presets: dolly in (the default), dolly out, pan left/right, tilt up/down, orbit left/right, crane up, Ken Burns. Each eases in and out and zooms just enough that no border shows.
- Amount: an `intensity` passed in is the user's choice and is used as given. Left out (an amount the app picks), each still gets 0.5, or less where 0.5 is predicted to smear it or bend its straight lines (below), but not below 0.25. Every move returns `intensity` (the amount used), `safe` (`{ smear, bend }`: the largest amounts predicted not to smear and not to bend this still with this preset), `safeIntensity` (the smaller of the two), `smearRisk` (`intensity > safe.smear`) and `bendRisk` (`intensity > safe.bend`), so the app can warn about a strong setting instead of lowering it. With `alternatives` (the other presets the user can pick), a move whose preset bends the still's lines even at 0.25 also returns `alwaysBends`: every alternative bends them below 0.25 too, so no other choice helps. `safeCameraAmounts(media, project, clipId, preset?)` gives `safe` for a clip's existing move from its stored depth image, for that warning when the strength changes later, and `safeCameraIntensity` the smaller of the two.
- Off the page's thread: the depth preparation and the prediction (`cameraPrediction.ts`: the still's straight lines and contrast, then the smear and bend searches) run in the camera worker (`workers/camera.worker.ts`), which caches the scenes of the last 4 stored depth images. The app starts that worker when the browser is first idle (`startCameraWorker`), because a worker's script is fetched only when the worker starts: a move made after the network drops still finds it. Without workers (unit tests), and where the worker's script does not load (offline before it ever started), they run on the calling thread. In Chrome on a 4-core VM, on the 6 dev stills measured (3 runs each), no task on the page's thread reached 50 ms while a move was added, while the safe amounts of a still were first computed, or while they were computed for another preset. Before, the longest task was 118–182 ms while a move was added and up to 91 ms for the safe amounts.
- Smear: where the camera reveals background that a near object covered, the ray march fills it by stretching the background next to the object, which shows as streaks on a detailed background (a mountainside behind branches, trees behind a fence). `smearOf(scene, move)` (`render/smear.ts`) is the share of the frame where the warp stretches a detailed part of the still to more than twice the move's zoom, at the start and end of the move, where every preset moves most. `safe.smear` is the largest amount (in steps of 0.05) under `SMEAR_LIMIT`, 0.5 % of the frame: on the dev stills, moves under 0.3 % looked clean and moves over 1 % showed streaks or bent lines. Its ray march is a CPU copy of the shader's and matches the renderer pixel for pixel (`tests/e2e/vision-media.spec.ts`). In the browser it takes 16–99 ms per still and preset (median 41 ms on the 35 dev stills, depth images about 780 × 518).
- Bend: Depth Anything gives each window of a facade and each plank of a fence a depth of its own, and a move turns that into waves along a tower's edges, window rows and fence wires. Nothing stretches there, so the smear prediction does not see it. `straightLines(gray)` (`render/bend.ts`) finds the still's straight edges at least 4.2 % of the width long, once per still (19–79 ms in the browser, median 39 ms). `bendOf(scene, move)` is the length of those lines, as a share of the frame width, that the move pushes more than 3 px (at 1920 wide) off a straight course within any tenth of the width, at the start and end of the move. Across an outline only the nearer side counts, since it moves as one, and a sudden step (something in front crossing the line) is not a bend. `safe.bend` is the last amount, going up from 0.05 in steps of 0.05, before `bendOf` passes `BEND_LIMIT`, 0.25 of the width: on the 35 dev stills (7 of them cityscapes), towers, planks and fence wires waved visibly from about 0.35, and around 0.2 the waves were small. The bend search takes 0–42 ms per still and preset in the browser (median 3 ms); the longest are on stills with many lines that never bend, where it runs all 20 steps.
- Depth preparation (`prepareDepthForParallax`) grows near regions past the object's edge and blurs the step into a ramp, from which the revealed background is filled. Beside a large near region (a person, an umbrella) the ramp is short (0.7 % dilation, 0.25 % blur), so straight background lines stay straight up to the silhouette. Around thin near structures (narrower than about 2.4 % of the width: branches, wires, railings) it is about 2 % wide (1.5 % dilation, 1 % blur), so a tangle of twigs moves as one layer instead of streaking the background between them. Thin means no point of its own near region within 1.2 % of the width (counted in steps that stay inside the region) lies deeper inside it than that, so a small near structure next to a large one is still thin. About 110 ms per still on CPU (about 130 ms in the browser), in the camera worker.
- Measured on 25 Wikimedia Commons stills (not in the repository): 15 with branches, fences, railings or foliage in front of a detailed background and 10 portraits, crowds and landscapes. At dolly in 0.5, 4 of the 15 smear more than 0.5 % of the frame (13 of 15 with a short ramp everywhere; left to the app they get 0.25 to 0.4), and 2 of the 10 others (they get 0.3 and 0.45). At orbit 0.5: 5 of 15 and 3 of 10. At amount 1 most stills with occluders smear (dolly in 12 of 15, orbit left 13 of 15) and 7 or 8 of the 10 others do.
- With the bend limit, on 35 dev stills (Wikimedia Commons, not in the repository: 19 with occluders, 3 portraits, 2 crowds, 4 landscapes, 7 cityscapes), the automatic amount stays the same on every preset for 24 of them. Dolly in drops for 10 (the 4 cityscapes with towers close up from 0.45 or 0.5 to 0.25, a bridge, a shed, a fence and a portrait in front of wooden walls to 0.25 or 0.3, and two by 0.05), orbit for 10, pan for 9, Ken Burns for 8, crane up for 3, tilt for none. At dolly in 0.25, 6 of them still bend more than the limit (3 cityscapes, the bridge, the shed and the fence); the app says so.
- The effect works in the still's own coordinates (`u_canvasToLayer`), so it follows the clip's fit and any position, scale or rotation, including keyframed ones; the camera frames the part of the still that is on the canvas. Without a depth map it still pans and zooms, with no parallax.
- The pose depends only on the params and progress, so the effect computes it once per frame on the CPU with `cameraPoseAt` (`frameUniforms`) instead of in every pixel.
- `createParallaxRenderer()` draws the same camera outside the compositor: it shares the shader's ray march and takes a `cameraPoseAt` pose (the effect and the renderer are compared per preset in `tests/e2e/vision-media.spec.ts`).
- Known limits: close-up towers, plank walls and fences can still bend at 0.25, the lowest automatic amount; the app warns there instead of going lower, and where every Motion bends them below 0.25 (`alwaysBends`) it says so once instead of suggesting another Motion or a lower Strength. The prediction can miss a ripple, and then there is no warning: on a fresh set of 15 stills the builders had not seen, the background behind a row of trees and lamp posts rippled in 4 of the 5 Motions at the default amount on one still. The automatic amount dropped on all 4 (by 0.05 to 0.25), the ripple stayed about as strong, and 3 of the 4 were predicted not to bend, so no warning showed (inferred from the stored amounts). A line that something in front crosses at one amount and not the next makes the bend jump between amounts, which is why `safe.bend` stops at the first step over the limit. A chain-link fence or a row of posts close to the camera over a detailed background still smears at 0.5 (posts that far apart do not merge into one layer); the automatic amount drops for such stills, a user-set one does not. The edge of a large near object over a detailed background streaks a little on moves of 0.5 and more; the prediction counts it, so the automatic amount drops there too. Strong moves (amount near 1) smear behind most foreground objects. A faint halo around the subject. A clip whose move uses a depth image from an older preparation keeps it until a camera move is added to that clip again. The ray march takes up to 48 depth reads per pixel.
